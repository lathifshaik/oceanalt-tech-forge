# How AI voice agents and AI receptionists are sold to small businesses

Researcher: `researcher` agent. All pages checked **8 October 2026**. Prices go stale fast, so recheck before quoting any of them.

**Method and limits (read first)**
- Each homepage or pricing page was fetched as text. Text extraction can't see layout, colour or animation, so the "visual style" notes are limited to what the page text, labels and asset names show. Where I couldn't tell, I say so. Before copying any design pattern, look at the pages in a browser.
- I checked whether each page **states** that callers are told they're speaking to an AI, or that calls are recorded. "Not stated" means the page doesn't say. It doesn't mean the product never discloses.
- Prices are shown in the currency the page uses. USD prices are not converted to AUD.
- **F** = fact taken from the cited page. **I** = my inference.

## 3. Synthesis

### The 5 most common and most convincing patterns (I, based on the profiles above)
1. **Let the visitor hear it, live.** Call a demo number (Rosie, Dialzara, Upfirst, Smith.ai, AI Phones, VOCPhone), have it call you (Sophiie, Retell), or talk in the browser (Waboom, Trillet, Bland). Almost every serious seller offers one of these. Goodcall and Synthflow don't, and their pages feel the weakest for it.
2. **Short recorded sample calls labelled by job type** ("Electricians 1:09" on Sophiie, Rosie's four scenarios, Upfirst's "Listen · 1:12"). It's lower effort than a live call, and it works with the sound off if the transcript is shown too.
3. **The transcript shown next to what happened.** Trillet's action log, Bland's tool steps, Sophiie's activity feed ("New booking confirmed for 1pm"), the SMS summary on Upfirst and Smith.ai. Buyers want to see what they *receive*, not only the conversation.
4. **Naming the trade and the moment.** AI Phones ("You're on the tools. The phone rings."), Sophiie's trade list, TransferToAI and Waboom's "for tradies" pages. Generic pages (Goodcall, ElevenLabs) talk about platforms, not jobs.
5. **Low-risk entry and visible price per minute or call.** "Cancel anytime", 7 to 28-day trials or money-back, published overage rates (Trillet $0.20/min, Dialzara $0.48/min, Waboom 95c/min with a 30-second example).

### What Oceanalt is missing
- **A way to hear it.** Our Story shows an animated transcript, which is good, but there's no voice. Nothing to listen to, no number to ring. Every strong competitor has at least one. (I)
- **A worked cost example in the price.** We already say "200 minutes is roughly $30 to $40" (F, `src/seo/landing.ts`). Copy Waboom and add a single-call example ("a 3-minute call costs about 50 to 60 cents", at our 15 to 20c/min estimate). (I)
- **Urgent versus non-urgent handling.** VOCPhone routes urgent calls to your mobile. Our concierge transcript covers the triple zero line, but the offer copy doesn't say "urgent jobs come straight through to you". (I) Only add this once the concierge actually does it.
- **The job software tradies use.** Sophiie, TransferToAI and Chime Labs all lead with ServiceM8, Simpro, Tradify or Fergus. The CLAUDE.md logo rule says only tools we actually use or connect to, so only list these once we really connect. (I)
- **A trial or guarantee that answers "cancel anytime".** Our test-calls-first promise is good but buried. Say it next to the price. (I)
- **A sample-call artefact:** what the owner receives (the WhatsApp/SMS summary card). We have it in the story (Jim's WhatsApp). Make it visible in the concierge landing page too. (I)

### What NOT to copy
- **"Never misses a call" style guarantees** (Dialzara's hero, "captures every lead"). Calls can drop, numbers can fail and forwarding can be misconfigured. An absolute promise is risky under the ACL ban on false or misleading representations. Say "answers when you can't" instead. (I; ACL s29 per ACCC guidance, https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-releases-guidance-about-online-product-reviews, checked 8 Oct 2026)
- **"Callers can't tell it's AI" as a selling point** (Dialzara, AI Phones, Bland's quote, Waboom's opt-in disclosure). This conflicts with our Terms (`src/App.tsx`: the concierge "tells every caller they're speaking with an AI assistant"). Australia's Guidance for AI Adoption (National AI Centre, Oct 2025) includes "disclose your use of AI" under "Share essential information" (Allens summary, https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2025/11/governance-doesnt-stand-still-9-faqs-to-help-understand-the-governments-new-guidance-for-ai-adoption/, checked 8 Oct 2026; primary: https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/guidance-for-ai-adoption).
- **Recording without a notice.** Several US sellers record every call without saying callers are told. In Australia, recording falls under state and territory surveillance devices laws plus the federal Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act. NSW is described as stricter, so keep the opening notice. (Secondary legal sources: https://www.sprintlaw.com.au/articles/is-it-legal-to-record-a-phone-call-in-australia, https://www.sydneycriminallawyers.com.au/?p=46816, checked 8 Oct 2026. Not legal advice; get a lawyer to check the greeting wording.)
- **Unsourced industry stats and savings figures** ("16 hrs+ saved", "$42K average yearly savings", "80% lower cost", the "0%" placeholder stats on Dialzara, Chime's "270,000 missed calls a day" estimate, AIssie's 180-call test). If we ever cite a missed-call figure, it must have a named, checkable source and be labelled.
- **ROI calculators built on made-up inputs** (My AI Front Desk links several). If we build one, the visitor enters their own numbers and nothing is pre-filled as a claim. (I)
- **Self-reported counters and borrowed testimonials** ("Trusted by 10,000+ businesses", Waboom's only testimonial being from a realtor on a tradie page). The ACCC says it's illegal to create fake or misleading reviews, and that testimonials must be genuine (s29). We have no concierge clients yet, so no counters and no testimonials, only labelled samples. https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-releases-guidance-about-online-product-reviews (checked 8 Oct 2026)
- **A US demo number on an Aussie pitch** (AI Phones). If we add a demo line, it should be an Australian number. (I)
- **"The Best" or "#1" claims** (Sophiie, Retell). These are unprovable comparative claims. (I)

### The demo that would convince an Aussie tradie in 10 seconds (I)
**A tap-to-call Australian demo number for a clearly labelled sample business ("Kerr & Sons Electrical, a sample business, ring it and book a job"), with the WhatsApp summary arriving on screen or by SMS when you hang up.** In the moment it's: call, hear the AI disclosure and the Aussie voice, get booked, see the summary land. That matches the strongest patterns found (a live demo number on AI Phones, Dialzara and Smith.ai, "Be difficult. Try to break it." on AI Phones, and the SMS follow-up on Smith.ai). It also proves the disclosure is natural rather than awkward.

For people who won't ring a stranger's number (on site, or at night), back it up with a **30 to 60-second recorded sample call** played over our existing animated transcript, captioned so it works on mute.

A browser talk widget (Waboom, Bland) is a good second choice, but tradies are on their phones and a mic-permission prompt adds friction. (I)

Conditions before building:
- The demo line must be the real product.
- It must say it's AI and that the call is recorded.
- It must be labelled as a sample.
- Its running cost needs a cap.
- It must not collect the caller's details beyond what's needed to send the demo summary, with consent.
- Any SMS sent must follow the Spam Act rules in `docs/growth/OUTREACH.md`.
