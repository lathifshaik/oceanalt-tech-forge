# Studio study: how premium studios present themselves

Why: Oceanalt is moving from "websites for small businesses" to a design and technology studio for established Australian brands, quoted per project, no prices shown. The founder pointed at Hypr (hypragency.com). This note looks at Hypr and 20 other studios, captured November 2026 at desktop (hero, 30% and 60% scroll) and mobile. Mood and patterns only. Nothing here is to be copied.

Capture notes: the Toko capture landed on an unrelated Swedish blog, and the "Re" capture is Re: Agency (London and New York), not the Sydney studio, so neither counts as Australian evidence. Manual's copy uses US spelling ("organizations"), so treat it as a reference, not a local voice.

## 1. The first screen

- **One statement, or just the name.** Collins: "Rewrite your worth." (three words). For The People: "People deserve better brands." Frost and Instrument show only the wordmark, edge to edge. Ragged Edge shows its name over a blurred colour field. The longest is Manual, one sentence across three lines.
- **Type is huge or quiet, never in between.** Big: Instrument, Frost, DesignStudio ("FURTHER" in a display serif), For The People (custom caps). Quiet: Collins (one serif line, lots of air), Koto ("We're Koto, The Creative Company" in the bottom corner over film).
- **Work beats words.** Pentagram, Mucho, Locomotive, Fantasy, Hardhat and Studio Dumbar open on a full-bleed image or film from a real project, captioned small (Pentagram names the project and one line on it). Studio Brave puts its own project grid inside a browser frame on a brick wall.
- **Thin nav.** Three to five links: Work, About/Studio, Services, Contact. Pentagram, Hardhat (Work, Team, Contact), Locomotive ("Work, Agency, Careers, Store"). Collins, Fantasy and Manual hide it behind one menu button.
- **Quiet call to action.** Locomotive's "Let's talk" in the corner, Mucho's outlined "Contact", Fantasy's "Let's talk. We'd love to hear from you." at the bottom of the page. Hypr is the outlier: two buttons ("Book a call", "View Case Studies") plus a trust line and an urgency bar above the nav.

## 2. Work, services, process, people, proof

- **Work.** Three forms recur.
  - A big captioned grid: Re: Agency (four across, small labels), Pentagram (three across, title and one line), Hello Monday.
  - A typographic index: Locomotive lists client names as huge serif lines with rules between them ("Wolverine Worldwide", "The Drake Hotel"... "All Work").
  - Dark bands of case-study cards: Manual (image, client, one-line outcome in a serif), Hypr ("See how our clients are changing their businesses", a sideways scroll), Koto (black, one project at a time).
- **Services** sit low and plain. Collins lists four programmes as rows (name left, one line right). Studio Brave ends on "See all Branding / See all Digital". Hypr uses tall cards per channel. Frost puts its three practices (Brand, Place, Environments) in the nav.
- **Process** is short and rarely a diagram. Hypr's "From shoot day to live ads in one loop" is a five-step stack next to one paragraph, the clearest version we saw. Most studios skip process and let case studies carry it.
- **People.** Hardhat names leaders by role on a dark page, then one line on partnership. Manual and Ragged Edge close with "Join us" and a hiring line. Collins and Locomotive link Team or Careers in the nav.
- **Proof** is the work itself, plus earned awards (Collins shows a row of real AdAge and D&AD laurels; Instrument a small "Design Studio Agency of the Year" line). Mucho's "250+ brands" is the only stat we saw outside Hypr, and Ragged Edge shows its B Corp mark.

## 3. Type, colour, spacing, motion

- **Type.** One display face doing a lot of work, paired with a plain sans. Serifs signal craft (Collins, Manual, Locomotive's condensed serif, Hardhat's leadership page). Heavy grotesques signal confidence (Frost, Instrument). Several studios commission or draw their own (For The People, Studio Dumbar).
- **Colour.** Mostly black, white and an off-white or warm stone (Collins, Re: Agency, Studio Brave). Colour arrives through the work, not the interface. When a studio does use colour it is one loud field (Locomotive red, Instrument acid yellow, Studio Dumbar magenta).
- **Spacing.** Very generous. A section often holds one sentence or one image. Small uppercase labels with letterspacing do the wayfinding (Hardhat, DesignStudio, Hypr's "WHY ONE TEAM").
- **Motion.** Film loops in the hero (Koto, Fantasy, Hardhat), 3D type (Studio Dumbar), a carousel (Obys). Mood, then out of the way.
- **What they avoid.** No prices anywhere. No feature badges, star ratings, "100% satisfaction", stock photos or icon grids. No chat widgets. Footers are short: address, email, phone, socials, newsletter (Locomotive, Ragged Edge, Frost).

## 4. Australian studios

Hardhat (Melbourne), Frost* (Sydney), For The People (Sydney), Christopher Doyle & Co (Sydney) and Studio Brave (Melbourne).

- **Plain, confident, a bit cheeky.** For The People asks "Why do all the cars, phones, and buildings look the same?" Hardhat calls its guide "Cheat codes for challenger brands" and its work "Clickin' awesome". Frost lets the asterisk speak.
- **Less sales, more studio.** Christopher Doyle's home page is three words (CD&CO., Projects, Profile), a two-line bio and the street address in Surry Hills. Brave's work is labelled "Living spaces" and "Framing a secret garden", not "results".
- **Place matters.** Real addresses and phone numbers up front (Doyle, Brave, Frost). Australian landscape in the work (For The People's Meander Valley identity over misty Tasmanian hills).
- **Country and conduct.** For The People has an Acknowledgement of Country in the footer. Frost links its Reconciliation Action Plan. It reads as normal for a serious Australian studio, not as a badge.

## 5. What Oceanalt adopts

- One short statement in our own words, set big. A sentence at most.
- Real work (or clearly labelled sample work) as the hero image, captioned small with the project and one line.
- Three to five nav links: Work, Services, Studio, Contact. One quiet "Start a project" link, not a stack of buttons.
- Case studies as dark bands or a large captioned grid, each card: client, what we made, one line on what it does.
- Services as plain rows (brand identity, websites, web apps and software, AI agents), equal weight, one line each. "Quoted per project" said once, plainly.
- A short process like Hypr's loop: four or five steps, one paragraph, no diagram beyond that.
- People: Lathif's face, name and role, and a straight line about who answers the phone.
- Off-white and black with one accent, one display face plus Geist, generous space, small letterspaced labels.
- Film or motion only where it shows real work, and it stops for reduced motion.
- An Acknowledgement of Country in the footer, plus street-level location and a phone number.

## What we don't copy

- Hypr's "Limited spots remaining for the Q4 intake" bar. No scarcity, ever.
- "Trusted by 100+ clients", Mucho's "250+ brands", or any count we can't back with a list.
- Client logo strips or testimonials we don't have. Hypr runs both; we show neither until they're real and signed off.
- Prices, "What does it cost?" answered with numbers, or plan tables.
- Awards rows (Collins) until we have awards.
- Anyone's wordmark treatment, custom typeface, layout or wording. Frost's giant name, For The People's letterforms and Locomotive's index are theirs.
- 3D and WebGL showpieces (Studio Dumbar, Obys) that bury the message and slow phones down.
- US sales voice ("ambitious ideas", "command their unique premium"). We write like Australians: short, dry, specific.
