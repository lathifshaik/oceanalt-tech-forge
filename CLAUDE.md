# Oceanalt

Oceanalt is run as a small firm of Claude agents plus the founder. It sells three things to Australian small businesses, with equal weight: **digital presence** (website, Google profile, SEO; Care $29; Launch: month one just the domain at cost, then $69/month to month 6 and $99 from month 7; Grow $149; 6-month minimum, then the site is theirs; or one-off from $1,499), **web apps and software** (quoted per project), and **AI agents** ($39/month assistant + usage at cost, custom agents quoted). Tagline: "More customers. Less admin."

What we sell, in the owner's words: **get found** (website, Google Business Profile, SEO, showing up in AI search), **when you can't pick up** (AI concierge $149/month with a local number + call time at cost; website chat assistant $39/month), **less admin** (we make their pipeline efficient, from first enquiry to paid invoice: web apps and workflow automation, quoted per project). Client sites and apps are hosted on Cloudflare and AWS, and client data stays in Australia (AWS Sydney); keep that true (see OPERATIONS.md).

Every service on the site should feel like a demo: `src/components/Story.tsx` has the hero phone, the call and the checkout; `src/components/Demos.tsx` has the pipeline demo and the logo wall (brand SVGs in `public/logos/`, logos only, names in alt text; only tools we actually use or connect to, never WordPress, Wix or Squarespace); the story has the animated concierge call and checkout. Demos use sample businesses and say so.

**We sell a story, to Australians.** Think like a marketer: lead with the moment the owner recognises ("you're up a ladder and the phone rings"), then what changes, then the product. Write the way Aussie small-business owners talk: plain, direct, a bit dry, Australian spelling, no hype or American sales talk, local words where they fit naturally (tradie, sparky, arvo, on the tools) but never forced. Aussies trust straight answers: show prices, say what's not included, never promise rankings or invent stats.

## The 15-minute reply (`api/start.ts`)
A Vercel function: the start form posts to it, Claude recommends a plan and layout as structured output, and Resend emails the reply to the enquirer and the lead to us. It falls back to the Firebase/EmailJS path if its env vars aren't set. Internally Oceanalt runs on AI agents, checked by people, but on the site we don't say the sites are built by AI (founder's call, 12 Oct 2026): lead with the founder, Lathif Shaik, who designs, signs off every site and answers the phone (his card by the start form, his name in the footer; his photo is `public/team/lathif.webp` via `FOUNDER_PHOTO` in `src/App.tsx`, and he is titled "Builder"). Don't mention AI in the trust, start-form or footer copy (founder's call). Keep it true: a person reads every enquiry the same business day. The site only promises the 15-minute reply once this function runs on Cloudflare (where the site is hosted) and passes an end-to-end test; until then it promises a same-business-day reply from a person.

Publicly we call this **custom design, never reused**; don't call it "templates" in client-facing copy. `npm run templates` builds the examples into `public/work/` for the portfolio (this runs automatically before `dev` and `build`). Read `templates/README.md` before building a client site. Design references collected in Chrome live in `docs/design/references/` (how: `docs/design/REFERENCE_BOARDS.md`). Four sample businesses are designed in the poster themes only (`example-bakery`, `example-architects`, `example-plants`, `example-garden`: Lemon & Rye, Small Spaces Architects, Greenhouse Plant Co, Oakleaf Gardens); the older samples keep their original designs. Three beauty samples (`studio/example-hair`, `studio/example-skin`, `shop/example-skincare`) use blush and sage. Twelve more samples cover a gym, barber, dentist, tutor, vet, mechanic, cleaner, real estate agency, accountant, wedding photographer, early learning centre and winery, each in its own new theme plus one other. Every layout has marked sections (`sections` reorders them) and section variants (`look`, e.g. treatments as a menu, tiles or numbered list; see `templates/README.md`), so designs differ below the hero, not just in the hero and colours (founder: "everything looks alike under", 14 Oct 2026). ## Selling the AI concierge (CEO decisions, 8 Oct 2026)
- The product is the **AI concierge** on the site. On calls it introduces itself as **"[owner]'s AI agent"**, never a human name, and every call opens with the AI and recording notice (can't be switched off). Never "receptionist" in our copy; "AI receptionist" only in the landing meta description, because buyers search it.
- Never "never miss a call" or any promise that every call is answered. Urgent transfer is "can … if you want".
- Show the cost per call (15 to 20c/min estimate, a 3-minute call about 45 to 60 cents) and put "3-month minimum. Nothing is charged until it passes our test calls" next to the price.
- "Hear a sample call" player: the recording is `public/audio/concierge-sample.mp3`, made by `scripts/make-sample-call.py` from the lines in `src/data/sample-call.json`. Since 14 Oct 2026 it uses ElevenLabs on the paid Starter plan (licensed for commercial use; free-plan audio never is): model `eleven_v4`, Australian library voices Kate (`w9rPM8AIZle60Nbpw7nl`, Jim's AI agent) and Emma (`56bWURjYFHyYyVf490Dp`, Mel), run with `--engine elevenlabs --model eleven_v4 --agent <id> --caller <id>` and the key as the `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` secret. The player only renders when `audio` is set in that JSON, and its shown length is the real file length. A second call, a restaurant booking (`src/data/sample-call-restaurant.json`, `public/audio/concierge-restaurant.mp3`, made with `--data` and `--out`): Pauline (Jess, `ys3XeJJA4ArWMhRpcX1D`) books a table for six at Nonna Pina, and the agent is "Maria's AI agent" (Kate).
